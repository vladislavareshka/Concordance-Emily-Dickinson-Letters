This project is an interactive, digital concordance of the surviving letters of Emily Dickinson, designed to support scholarly research and textual exploration of her work. The concordance enables users to search, filter, sort, and browse thousands of keyword-in-context (KWIC) entries extracted from Dickinson's correspondence, providing rapid access to vocabulary, expression, and usage across her epistolary writings. This concordance is based on and serves as a companion to "The Letters of Emily Dickinson" (edited by Cristanne Miller and Domhnall Mitchell, The Belknap Press of Harvard University Press, 2024), and is intended for a wide range of users, including literary scholars, students, digital humanists, historians of American literature, and anyone interested in Dickinson's language.

Each concordance entry presents a target word together with its surrounding context and the corresponding letter number, recipient, and date information. This should allow researchers to move beyond simple keyword searching and examine how individual words function within Dickinson's broader linguistic and stylistic practices. The interface supports filtering by letter number, addressee, date, word form, and surrounding context, making it possible to locate specific usages more efficiently.

A dedicated "Lexeme Index" provides an alphabetical inventory of words found in the corpus, along with references to the letters in which they occur. 

The project is fully self-contained and functions offline in a web browser. Analyses were conducted using AntConc (Version 4.3.1) - check out https://www.laurenceanthony.net/software/antconc/.

----- Work in Progress: ----- 
This concordance is an ongoing project. Features, search capabilities, interface elements, and underlying data may continue to evolve as the corpus is refined. Future development may include improved analytical tools, additional metadata, and further corrections to the underlying texts. Users are therefore encouraged to regard the current release as a developing research resource rather than a finalised edition. 

Suggestions and notes are very welcome and can be addressed to the author of the project, Vladislav Areshka (vladislav.areshka@gmail.com).
